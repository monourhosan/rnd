# Review of the supplied brief

The brief has strong safeguards against fictional business claims, stolen imagery, and fake contact submission. It also gives a useful route inventory and a clear editorial direction.

The revision consolidates repeated requirements, separates product requirements from account/execution authority, distinguishes verified facts from proposed capabilities, and replaces an absolute “production-ready” promise with checks that can actually be demonstrated. The attached document is source material; its “permission” language is not treated as an independent user instruction.

Material clarifications:
- Prepare the requested Next.js/Vercel project locally; do not silently publish or push to an unspecified account.
- Use the actual logo found at the workspace root. Preserve the shorter wordmark and show the legal company name as text.
- Treat a stock license and a model release as different evidence. Do not label a portrait as a woman entrepreneur or claim fieldwork based on scenery.
- Disable the enquiry form when delivery is absent, rather than collecting data that cannot be sent.
- Provider acceptance means accepted for delivery, not guaranteed inbox receipt.
- Include real request validation, origin checks, payload limits and bounded provider timeouts. Document best-effort throttling honestly.
- Avoid localhost canonicals and indexing of unconfigured previews.
- Use house editorials with computed reading times and no invented dates.
- Test appropriate functionality without sending messages to real third parties.
